# iTruVoice 1.1.0

Ten Japanese voices, with the dictionary that makes them read Japanese.

**Taro, Tsuyoshi, Kenta, Daichi, Takeshi, Ojiisan, Osamu, Akira, Hanako and
Keiko**, filed under Japanese.

Japanese is not a decompilation: there is no Japanese TruVoice to reproduce.
The front end is built from the phonetics literature and drives the
synthesiser below stage 3, and it reads a 27 MB morphological dictionary,
`jadic.bin`, from disk. That file now ships in the app bundle, and the engine
is pointed at it from both the app and the provider extension.

Measured on the pinned engine at 16 kHz, Taro, with and without the file:

- `私は日本語を話します` without it: 14,880 samples, 0.93 s. Only the kana
  particles survive; every kanji is dropped.
- The same sentence with it: 37,280 samples, 2.33 s, exactly the length of the
  same sentence written in kana. The kanji read.
- `konnichiwa` without it: 52,960 samples, 3.31 s, because the ten Latin
  letters are spelled out. With it: 13,600 samples, 0.85 s, the romaji
  reading.

The host corpus check now runs six Japanese lines -- kana, kanji, numbers and
romaji -- against a recorded golden with the dictionary in place, so a bundle
that loses the file fails the build rather than shipping a voice that silently
drops kanji. Both workflows copy the file into the `.app` and verify its bytes
and its presence in the `.ipa`.

**Two things to know about the Japanese voices.** Akira carries 8 dB of source
attenuation to stay clean and is quieter than the rest. And 8 kHz output is
refused for Japanese -- its sibilants sit at and above that Nyquist -- so it
speaks at 11,025 or 16,000 Hz only; the app runs at 16 kHz.

Everything from 1.0.9 is unchanged: the eleven British voices, thirty-three
before these ten, and American English still byte-identical to the recorded
golden.
