# iTruVoice 1.0.9

Eleven British English voices, on a newly pinned engine.

Upstream tv-decomp `8488e51` (the British English commit, plus the Japanese
work and the tree reorganisation under it), measured against the built engine
on the host.

**British English, thirty-three voices total.** Nigel, Clive, Eager Edwin,
Deep Duncan, Rupert, Gramps Amos, Malcolm, Alistair, Fiona, Imogen and
Bernard appear under English (United Kingdom). They are the American
speakers, not new recordings: an accent moves the vowels relative to each
other and a voice in this engine is a vocal tract, so the same ten tracts
speak British, with stage 3's formants re-aimed at Cruttenden's General
British targets and the accent's phoneme substitutions applied. Each British
voice keeps the character of the American it shares a number with -- Peter is
Nigel, Deep Douglas is Deep Duncan, Wanda is Fiona, Julia is Imogen -- and
Frank, the deep voice, is Bernard.

**The American voices did not move.** The corpus golden is unchanged, line
for line and sample for sample, on the new engine: same counts, same audio.
A second corpus now runs on an en-GB synth and records its own counts, and
upstream's own `tests/gb_check.c` is built and run against the pinned tree:
on all six test phrases the British hashes differ from the American ones while
the American path stays untouched, and the /r/ linking, intrusive and refusal
rules hold. That check also reports one upstream failure at this commit -- the
phoneme string for "far off" comes out `&Fr1R&o1F.` where upstream wants
`&Fr1R%oF.` -- and upstream's own 64-bit build produces the same line, so it
is recorded here rather than papered over.

**Japanese is compiled and not offered.** The new engine carries a Japanese
language row, so its front end is in the link; its dictionary is 27 MB and
upstream reads it from disk at runtime, which is not vendored. The ten
Japanese voices therefore sit at 22 through 31 in the engine numbering and
are absent from the picker, which is why British English starts at 32.

**Your saved voices keep speaking the same person.** Identifiers still carry
the voice name, so nothing renumbers for you: Peter is still Peter, Pedro
still Pedro.

Verified by the host corpus check (English golden unchanged, new British
golden recorded after reading the diff), upstream's gb_check output recorded
and matching, the language latch pinned at `en 11, es 11, ja 10, en-GB 11`,
and the CoreChecks suite, all passing.
